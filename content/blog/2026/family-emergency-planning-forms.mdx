Students in the Suffolk Law School [Legal Innovation and Technology (LIT) Clinic](https://www.suffolk.edu/law/academics-clinics/clinics-experiential-opportunities/clinics/legal-innovation-and-technology-clinic)
started the Fall 2026 semester by building guided interviews to help families arrange care for children when a parent
is away. The twelve new forms are available on CourtFormsOnline.org now.

The site now offers forms for nine more states: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Georgia,
Maryland, New Jersey, North Dakota, Rhode Island, and West Virginia. There are also new forms for Maine and
Massachusetts, where we already offered tools.

## Planning for a family emergency

An urgent immigration need inspired these projects: helping families plan for the care of their children if a parent
is detained or deported. The same forms can also help anyone who needs to make care arrangements before leaving the
country temporarily for a work trip, a family visit, or other travel. A hospitalization or another unexpected
absence can raise many of the same questions.

A parent may need a trusted adult to talk to a child's school or make health care decisions while they are away.
The new interviews help families prepare caregiver authorizations, powers of attorney, and petitions for
guardianship. The right form depends on the state and the family's situation. Some arrangements involve a court;
others involve signing an authorization and following the form's instructions.

Our new [family emergency planning page](/family) lists these tools by state. You can read about the available forms
and start a guided interview. You can also find them on each state's page.

## Learning by doing

The LIT Clinic is now in its third year as a six-credit clinic, part of Suffolk's eight-year-old Legal Innovation and
Technology Lab.

Even a short form asks students to work through a series of legal judgments: who can sign, what decisions a caregiver
can make, and when the document takes effect. They then turn that research into questions and instructions a family
can follow. Once published, each interview stays available to families in the state it was built for, so a student's
work becomes a public tool that can keep helping people after the semester ends.

Students will continue to work on larger projects through the rest of the academic year, building on what they learned
from these first interviews.
